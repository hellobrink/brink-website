challenge:
  - "Oxfam America works across a large portfolio spanning climate change, gender justice and fairer economies. Its strategy was sound, but the leadership team wanted to be more effective at turning that strategy into impact on the ground."
  - "Internal silos were getting in the way. Teams that needed to collaborate weren't, strategic priorities and day-to-day work had drifted apart, and there was little shared clarity on who owned what, how progress was measured, or how to work as one organisation."
results:
  - "A transformative upgrade to behaviour and culture, and radical new ways of working that enable greater organisational impact."
  - "More clarity around vision and action, and more shared ownership of delivery."
  - "Clearer, easier decision-making."
  - "A closer, more collaborative and collegiate atmosphere - teams felt more unified and more joined up."
  - "Senior leadership observed more sharpness and focus in how teams think and work together."
approachIntro:
  - "The work centred on enabling honest conversations and introducing more iterative, adaptive approaches. That meant changing mindsets at leadership level, so the organisation could get comfortable testing its assumptions, learning from mistakes and experimenting with new ideas."
approach:
  - "**Enabling crucial conversations.** As outside observers we could broach uncomfortable topics and uncover new ground. By streamlining priorities, tackling bottlenecks and revising reporting and budgeting approaches, teams gained clarity, focus and a more positive, efficient way of delivering."
  - "**Progress through practice.** We introduced iterative ways of working, which first meant shifting attitudes at leadership level so the organisation could become comfortable testing assumptions, learning from mistakes and experimenting."
  - "**Bringing people together.** We linked immediate priorities back to the strategic ones - poverty, inequality and injustice - grouped related work into clearer 'buckets', and ran cross-team reviews so teams that don't normally work together could reflect and collaborate."
